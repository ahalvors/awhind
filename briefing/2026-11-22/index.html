Microsoft says attackers read admin contact details from its Entra Connect Health service. If you got the notice, here's what to do

Microsoft fixed a flaw in Entra Connect Health that exposed administrator email addresses and tenant details. No patch is needed on your end, but those admin inboxes are now likely phishing targets.

Microsoft is notifying organizations that attackers exploited a flaw in the Entra Connect Health API and got read-only access to tenant-level metadata, per BleepingComputer. Entra Connect Health is the cloud service admins use to monitor the on-premises identity setup that syncs into Microsoft 365. Microsoft says the flaw was introduced on July 23, found on Nov. 9 and fixed server-side on Nov. 19. It finished its investigation on Nov. 20 and began notifying affected customers through Azure Service Health.

What was exposed. According to Microsoft's notice, as reported by BleepingComputer:
- Email addresses of tenant technical contacts and administrator accounts
- Tenant IDs, display names, initial domain names, country and region
- Entra license tiers and directory sync status, including the last sync time
- Connect Health onboarding status
- An expired certificate Microsoft says was no longer in use

Microsoft says no passwords, password hashes, authentication tokens, valid keys, on-premises Active Directory details or Azure subscription information were exposed, and it found no unauthorized configuration changes. It has not said how many organizations were affected or who was behind it, per iSec News and BleepingComputer.

Why it matters if you're a small business. Microsoft says no customer update is required. The risk now is the follow-up: whoever pulled this data knows who your Microsoft 365 admins are and how your tenant is set up. Microsoft's own advice is to expect targeted phishing and social engineering aimed at those admin addresses. A convincing fake "Microsoft security notice" sent to your admin is exactly what this kind of data makes easier.

Action items.
1. Check whether you got the notice. Microsoft sends tenant security notifications to Global Admins, Technical Contacts and Security Admins by email and Azure Service Health, per Microsoft Learn. If an IT provider manages your tenant, ask them. BleepingComputer reports some providers got notices for several customer tenants.
2. Make sure those contact addresses are current. Microsoft Learn says these addresses are where security notices go. A former employee's inbox or an unwatched mailbox means you miss the next one.
3. Warn your admins. Any email about this incident, or any "urgent Microsoft security" message, should be checked by signing in to the admin center directly, not by clicking a link in the email.
4. Use phishing-resistant MFA on admin accounts. Microsoft recommends it for privileged accounts. That means passkeys or security keys, not text-message codes.
5. Review your Entra audit and sign-in logs for admin sign-ins you don't recognize, as Microsoft advises.
6. Review your Connect Health settings. Microsoft published a PowerShell script at aka.ms/ConnectHealth/scripts that shows what kinds of tenant metadata could have been exposed. BleepingComputer notes it shows your current settings, not the data that was actually accessed.
