Attackers are using a SonicWall VPN flaw that can skip your multi-factor sign-in. If your office VPN runs on a SonicWall, check the firmware this week

SonicWall says a flaw in its SSL-VPN can let someone with a working password get past SAML sign-in, including the multi-factor step it was supposed to enforce. CISA lists it as exploited and gave federal agencies until Friday, Nov 20, to fix it. Here's how to tell if your firewall is affected and what to do.

A lot of small offices connect remote staff through a SonicWall firewall's SSL-VPN, and a lot of them added multi-factor sign-in through Microsoft Entra, Okta or another identity provider using SAML. A flaw SonicWall disclosed on Nov 6 can undo that second step. SonicWall says it investigated a report of the flaw being exploited, and CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list the same day, with a Nov 20 deadline for federal agencies, per CISA and BleepingComputer.

What the flaw does. The bug, CVE-2026-19868, lets a user with valid credentials for another configured sign-in method bypass SAML authentication on the SSL-VPN, and possibly the multi-factor check SAML was enforcing, per SonicWall's advisory SNWLID-2026-0014. The attacker still needs a working password. The problem is that the step meant to stop a stolen password may not run.

Who is affected. Only SonicWall firewalls with SAML turned on for SSL-VPN logins are exposed to this one. Affected versions are Gen 7 SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and older and Gen 8 SonicOS 8.2.2-8015 and older. SonicWall lists the fixes as 7.3.4-7013 or later for Gen 7 and 8.2.3-8015 or later for Gen 8, and says there is no workaround, per the advisory. The same advisory fixes six other SonicOS flaws, including two in the management page, so update even if you don't use SAML.

One detail worth knowing. On Gen 7, the exact build matters. ThreatListPro notes that a second SonicWall advisory, SNWLID-2026-0021, lists 7.3.4-7013 as affected by a separate certificate issue, and that 7.3.4-7015 is also the first 7.3.4 build for the virtual NSv models, per ThreatListPro. Read the release notes for your model in MySonicWall before you upgrade.

Older boxes. Gen 6 firewalls are not listed in this advisory, but SonicWall lists them as end of support, so they get no new fixes, per WaterISAC's notice on the flaw. If your office still runs one, it's time to plan the replacement.

Action items for this week.
1. Find out what's in the closet. Note the firewall model and the SonicOS version on its dashboard. If someone else manages it, ask them for both.
2. Update the firmware. Gen 7 to 7.3.4-7013 or later (check whether your model should go to 7.3.4-7015), Gen 8 to 8.2.3-8015 or later. Schedule a few minutes of downtime after hours.
3. Check recent VPN logins. WaterISAC advises comparing successful SSL-VPN logins against your identity provider's sign-in log. A VPN login with no matching sign-in and MFA check is a red flag. Reset that account's password and review what it reached.
4. Turn off sign-in methods you don't use. The flaw depends on a second working login method. If the VPN should only take SAML sign-ins, remove local or other accounts that can still log in.
5. Keep the management page off the internet. Two of the other fixed flaws sit in the SonicOS management interface, and New York State's IT office warns that internet-reachable management pages face more risk, per NY ITS advisory 2026-126.
