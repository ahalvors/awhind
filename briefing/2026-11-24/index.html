N-able's Mail Assure email filter was breached, and the hole is patched. If your IT provider uses it, here's what to ask

N-able says an attacker got into one server behind its Mail Assure and SpamExperts hosted email filtering service, which many IT providers run for their clients. The flaw is fixed, email kept flowing, and customer API tokens were revoked. Here's what that means for your business.

N-able says an unauthorized party accessed one of the servers behind Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud, an email filtering service it runs for managed service providers. On Nov. 16 the company took the management portal and API offline and suspended logging, mailbox synchronization and quarantine, while email delivery kept working, SecurityWeek reports. Two days later it confirmed a cyberattack: the attackers hit a web server and likely accessed certain credentials stored there.

What N-able fixed. During its investigation, N-able found and patched a critical flaw, CVE-2026-134624 (CVSS 4.0 score 9.3). A connection check used for LDAP synchronization did not restrict where it could connect, so an API user with enough privileges could make the server reach internal hosts and send crafted data to internal services. Build 20261121.1943 fixes it. N-able says it restored dashboard and API access on Nov. 21 at 23:45 UTC, and that inbound and outbound mail and spam filtering kept running throughout, per its incident update. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Nov. 23.

What N-able says, and what it hasn't said yet. The company says it has found no evidence of exploitation beyond the single N-able-hosted server, has brought in an outside forensics team, and will publish a formal root cause analysis within two weeks of its Nov. 22 update. The Hacker News reports N-able has found no evidence that customer data, including tenants' mailboxes, was compromised. N-able has not said how the attackers first got in or whether this flaw was the way in. It also says this incident is unrelated to the N-central incident earlier this month.

What N-able already did for customers. It took the affected server offline, rotated internal platform credentials, revoked customers' existing API tokens, invalidated OAuth tokens and reset local users' passwords, The Hacker News reports. Hosted customers have nothing to install. The hosted platform is already patched.

Why it matters if you're a small business. You may never have heard the name Mail Assure, because your IT provider runs it for you. It sits in front of your mailboxes and decides what is spam. If your provider uses it, some of the service, like quarantine and logging, may still be coming back in phases. Any scripts or integrations your provider built on the old API tokens need new credentials before they work again.

Action items for this week.
1. Ask your IT provider whether your email filtering runs on N-able Mail Assure or SpamExperts. If the answer is no, you're done with this one.
2. If yes, ask whether they received N-able's setup-specific guidance. N-able says it emailed each partner instructions for its environment and asks anyone who didn't get them to contact support.
3. Ask whether quarantine and logging are fully back. If a real message you expected never showed up, check with your provider before assuming it was never sent.
4. Expect a password reset if your staff use a quarantine login. N-able reset local users' passwords. Our own advice: reset through the portal address your provider gives you, not a link in an email, because attackers love fake "messages held in quarantine" emails.
5. If you run SpamExperts Local Cloud on your own servers, confirm you're on build 20261121.1943 or later. The incident statements describe the Hosted Cloud, and Local Cloud servers are yours to check.
6. Ask your provider to share N-able's root cause analysis when it's out. It should say how the attackers got in and whether any credentials were taken.
