    How the grade works

    This is AWHIND’s self-assessment rubric, not an independent audit, certification, compliance determination or automatic device scan.

    Each answer earns 2 points for tested and documented, 1 for in place but not fully tested, or 0 for absent/unknown. All 15 questions have equal weight. Unknowns stay in the denominator.

    A: 90–100 · B: 80–89 · C: 70–79 · D: 60–69 · F: below 60. If any of five core controls is not tested and documented, the score is capped at 69. All unknown answers produce “Unverified.”

    Core controls: enrollment, tamper/app controls, default-deny access, alternate-route coverage and off-campus protection. This rubric measures readiness for an allowlist-based environment.

    03 / PROVE IT BEFORE YOU EXPAND

    Five devices. Two weeks.
    A practical next step.

    BEFORE YOU BEGIN

    Set the boundaries.

    Name the school sponsor, instructional approver, and IT lead. Inventory essential lessons, accessibility needs, current tools and recovery procedures.

    WEEK ONE

    Build and validate.

    Configure five school-owned devices. Test sign-in, assigned content, assistive tools and submissions. Resolve narrow exceptions with the teacher.

    WEEK TWO

    Test the real world.

    Use the devices in class and at home. Verify alternate access paths, reboot behavior, and recovery. Record gaps before deciding whether to expand.

    Start with the tools your school already has

    Assess your current MDM and filtering licenses before replacing them. For MacBooks, evaluate school-controlled enrollment, device restrictions, managed browser policies and off-campus filtering together.

    Chrome Enterprise Core offers free browser management. Mosyle’s education plans include a free tier and paid capabilities. Jamf School is another education MDM option. If you use Securly, verify your licensed product’s always-on Mac coverage, rather than assuming a classroom site lock covers the whole day.

    Confirm current pricing and feature scope with each vendor. A browser allowlist is one layer; it does not establish whole-device protection. Google’s URL policy guidance · Apple’s filtering guidance.
